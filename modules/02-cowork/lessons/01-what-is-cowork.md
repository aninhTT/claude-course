# Lesson 2.01: What Is Cowork?

## Learn

In Module 1, you learned the difference between Chat, Cowork, and Claude Code. Now it's time to go deep on Cowork — your autonomous task executor.

### Cowork: Your Task Executor

Cowork is built on the same AI "brain" as Claude Code, but it's optimized for a different job: **autonomous task completion**. Where Claude Code is about collaborative building (you and Claude working together, step by step), Cowork is about delegation. You give it instructions, point it at the right context, and let it work.

Think of it this way: **Cowork is like delegating a task to a capable team member.** You wouldn't micromanage a good colleague — you'd give them clear instructions, make sure they have what they need, and let them do their thing. That's exactly how Cowork works.

Before diving in, watch this overview of Cowork to see it in action: https://www.loom.com/share/9700e223f40e4a408f07e0f1e1065cb1

### Giving Cowork Context

You can give Cowork context in a few ways:

- **Point it at a folder** — Cowork can read files in a folder to understand a project. This is optional, not required.
- **Upload documents** — You can upload files (Excel sheets, PDFs, docs) directly into a Cowork task. Great when you need it to work with specific data.
- **Use your connectors** — Cowork reads from Slack, email, Calendar, Granola, and other connected tools.

You don't always need a folder. Sometimes you just upload a document or point Cowork at your connectors and let it work.

### Cowork Reads From Your Connectors

This is where Cowork gets powerful. It can read from all the tools you connected in Module 1:

- **Slack** — search channels, read threads, find conversations
- **Gmail** — scan your inbox, read emails, find specific messages
- **Google Calendar** — check your schedule, see upcoming meetings
- **Granola** — access your meeting notes and transcripts
- **BigQuery** — query data directly

When you tell Cowork to "summarize what happened in Slack this week" or "find action items from my last 3 meetings," it reaches into these connectors to get the information it needs.

### How Cowork Differs From Just Chatting

The biggest difference between Cowork and Chat: Cowork **executes autonomously**. When you use Chat, it's a back-and-forth conversation — you ask, Claude responds, you refine. With Cowork, you set the task and it runs. It works through multiple steps, uses your connectors, and produces a deliverable — all without needing you to guide it at every turn.

This is why Cowork is the right choice for complex, multi-step work. A task like "compile all the decisions from this week's meetings and draft a summary" would take a dozen chat messages back and forth. In Cowork, it's one task instruction and Cowork handles it end to end.

Also important: **Cowork does NOT have plan mode.** Plan mode is a Claude Code feature (and you should always use it when building). Cowork just executes. This means your instructions need to be clear upfront because Cowork will take them and run.

### When to Use Cowork

Cowork is ideal when you need to:
- Pull together information from multiple sources
- Execute a well-defined task that doesn't need your input at every step
- Index and synthesize what happened across your tools
- Draft communications based on existing context
- Research a topic across your connected tools
- Prepare for meetings by compiling relevant information
- Analyze data and work with Excel spreadsheets or CSVs
- Execute multi-step complex workflows that would take too long in a back-and-forth chat

## Practice

Let's get hands-on with Cowork. This exercise is about getting comfortable with the interface.

**Step 1:** Open Cowork in the Claude app. Look at the **upper tabs** — you'll see options for Chat, Code, and **Cowork**. Click on Cowork, then hit **New Task** to start a fresh Cowork session.

> 💡 You can come back to this course conversation anytime — it's a separate session. Cowork tasks run independently. Here's a quick video showing how to navigate between your course session and Cowork: https://www.loom.com/share/692e1af8297d4b479fc1ec8972494f92

**Step 2:** Take a moment to explore the Cowork interface. Notice:
- Where you enter your task instructions
- How you can optionally point it at a folder or upload documents for context
- Where the output appears as Cowork works

**Step 3:** Give Cowork a simple instruction to get started. Try something like:

> "Summarize what Cowork can do for me. Include what tools and connectors it can access, and give me 5 example tasks I could use it for in my role."

**Step 4:** Watch how Cowork works through the task. Notice that it doesn't ask you questions — it just executes based on your instructions.

**Success criteria:** You've opened Cowork, explored the interface, and completed a simple interaction where Cowork produced output for you.
