# Lesson 2.02: Your First Cowork Task

## Learn

In the last lesson, you explored Cowork and gave it a simple instruction. Now it's time to run a **real task** — one that uses your connected tools and produces something genuinely useful.

### The Key Ingredients of a Good Cowork Task

Every good Cowork task has four ingredients:

1. **Clear objective** — What exactly should Cowork produce? "Summarize my week" is vague. "Create a bulleted summary of decisions made and action items assigned to me" is specific.

2. **Timeframe** (when relevant) — What period should Cowork look at? "The last 2 days," "since Monday," or "from March 1-5" all give Cowork clear boundaries. Not every task needs a timeframe, but most research and summary tasks do.

3. **Sources to check** — Which connectors should Cowork pull from? Be explicit: "Check my Slack messages, Gmail inbox, and Granola meeting notes." If you don't specify, Cowork may miss important sources or spend time on irrelevant ones.

4. **Output format** — What should the deliverable look like? "A bulleted summary," "a Slack message to myself," "a paragraph I can paste into our team standup" — the format matters.

**One more thing: point Cowork to the right documents.** If your task involves specific files — a spreadsheet, a brief, a report — upload them directly into the Cowork session or make sure they're in the folder you're pointing Cowork at. Cowork can only work with what it can see.

These four ingredients are a guide, not a rigid template. Pick and choose the elements that are relevant to your specific task. A data analysis task needs a file upload more than a timeframe. A research task needs sources more than an output format. Use your judgment.

### What Makes a Good Cowork Task?

A good Cowork task has a **specific scope** and a **clear deliverable**. Think of it like writing a ticket for a teammate:

**Good task:** "Check my Slack messages, Gmail, and Granola notes from the last 2 days. Find anything I need to know about or act on in my role as [your role]. Compile it into a summary organized by urgency — things that need action today, things to be aware of, and FYI items. Send the summary as a Slack message to me."

**Weak task:** "Tell me what I missed." (Too vague — missed where? Over what period? What counts as important?)

The more specific you are upfront, the better Cowork's output will be. Remember, Cowork doesn't have plan mode and it doesn't stop to ask clarifying questions — it takes your instructions and runs with them.

### What Cowork Produces

Cowork can produce output in several ways:
- Text directly in the Cowork session (you can copy from there)
- Slack messages sent to you or a channel
- Files saved to a folder
- Emails drafted or sent

For this first real task, we'll have Cowork send you a Slack message so you have a tangible deliverable.

## Practice

Let's run your first real Cowork task. Pick **one of the two options** below based on what's most useful to you right now:

### Option A: Catchup Summary (uses your connectors)

This task has Cowork index your recent activity and compile a personalized summary.

**Step 1:** Open a new Cowork session (upper tabs → Cowork → New Task).

**Step 2:** Give Cowork this task (customize the bracketed parts):

> "I need a catchup summary for the last 2 days. Here's what I need:
>
> **Who I am:** I'm a [your role, e.g., Product Manager on the Marketplace team].
>
> **Sources to check:**
> - My Slack messages and mentions (focus on channels relevant to my work)
> - My Gmail inbox (anything that needs a response or is time-sensitive)
> - My Granola meeting notes (key decisions and action items from any meetings)
>
> **What to include:**
> - Decisions that were made that I should know about
> - Action items assigned to me or my team
> - Questions or requests people sent me that I haven't responded to
> - Anything time-sensitive coming up
>
> **Format:** Organize by urgency:
> 1. Needs action today
> 2. Needs attention this week
> 3. FYI / good to know
>
> **Deliver:** Send this as a Slack message to me."

### Option B: Data Analysis (upload a file)

If you have a spreadsheet or data file you've been meaning to look at, this is a great first task.

**Step 1:** Open a new Cowork session (upper tabs → Cowork → New Task).

**Step 2:** Upload an Excel file, CSV, or any data file by dragging it into the Cowork session or using the upload button.

**Step 3:** Give Cowork a task like:

> "Analyze this data and give me:
> 1. A summary of what's in this file (rows, columns, what it represents)
> 2. The top 3 insights or trends you notice
> 3. Any data quality issues (missing values, outliers, inconsistencies)
> 4. Recommendations for what I should look at more closely
>
> Format the results as a clear, scannable summary."

### After either option:

**Review the output.** Ask yourself:

- Did it capture the important things?
- Did it miss anything?

**Success criteria:** You ran a Cowork task that either (a) indexed at least 2 connected sources and produced a summary, or (b) analyzed uploaded data and produced insights.

## Challenge

Now try a **multi-step Cowork task** — one where the output isn't just text in the session, but gets delivered somewhere.

**Step 1:** Give Cowork a task that produces a useful summary or analysis (use any of the prompts from this lesson or write your own).

**Step 2:** In the same task instructions, tell Cowork to take the output and **put it into a document** — have it create a markdown file or a formatted doc.

**Step 3:** Then tell Cowork to **send it to you on Slack** — either as a DM or posted to a channel.

This is a complex workflow: research → compile → format → deliver. It's the kind of multi-step task where Cowork really shines compared to Chat.

**Example prompt to get you started:**

> "Research what happened on my team this week by checking Slack channels and my email. Compile a weekly summary with highlights, decisions, and action items. Save it as a markdown document, and then send the summary to me as a Slack DM."

**Success criteria:** Ran a Cowork task that produced output AND delivered it somewhere (Slack message, document, or both).
