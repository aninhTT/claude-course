# Lesson 2.03: Your Solo Cowork Activity

## Learn

You've now seen how to write a clear Cowork task and run it successfully. The best way to get better at directing Cowork is to **use it for real work** — not course exercises, but actual tasks from your day.

This lesson is mostly practice. Here's a quick refresher on the principles before you dive in:

### The Cowork Task Checklist

Before you send any task to Cowork, make sure you've covered:

- **Specific objective** — Not "help me with X" but "produce Y by doing Z"
- **Defined scope** — Clear boundaries on timeframe, sources, and topic
- **Clear deliverable** — What should the output be? Where should it go?
- **Relevant sources** — Which connectors should Cowork check?

### Common First-Timer Mistakes

As you do your first solo task, watch out for these:

- **Too vague:** "Look at my stuff and tell me what's important" — Cowork doesn't know your priorities unless you tell it
- **Too broad:** "Analyze everything from the last month" — This takes forever and produces a wall of text. Narrow the scope.
- **No deliverable format:** If you don't specify how you want the output, you'll get a generic dump. Specify the format (bullets, paragraphs, table, Slack message, etc.)

### You Have Backup

Remember — you're in this course session right now with Claude Code. If you get stuck while working with Cowork, come back here and ask for help. Claude (in this session) can help you refine your Cowork instructions, troubleshoot issues, or brainstorm better prompts.

## Practice

This is your first **unguided Cowork task**. You pick the task, you write the instructions, you run it.

**Step 1: Pick a real task from your work.**

Choose something you actually need to get done or that would genuinely save you time. Here are some ideas, but use your own if you have one:

- **Briefing doc:** Compile a briefing document for an upcoming meeting by pulling from Slack, email, and past meeting notes
- **Email triage:** Have Cowork scan your inbox from the last 3 days and categorize emails by: needs response, FYI only, can archive
- **Slack research:** Research a specific topic or project across your Slack channels and compile the key points, decisions, and open questions
- **Meeting prep:** Prepare for a specific meeting by gathering all relevant context from your tools — what was discussed previously, open items, what you need to bring
- **Draft responses:** Have Cowork draft responses to 3-5 emails that are waiting in your inbox, using context from your other tools to make them informed
- **Weekly roundup:** Create a "what happened this week" summary for your team or manager

**Step 2: Write your Cowork instructions.**

Use the four ingredients: objective, timeframe, sources, output format. Be as specific as you can.

**Step 3: Open a new Cowork session and run it.**

Give Cowork your instructions and let it work. Don't intervene unless something goes clearly wrong.

**Step 4: Review the results.**

- Did Cowork deliver what you expected?
- Was the output useful as-is, or did it need editing?
- Were your instructions clear enough?

**Success criteria:** Completed a self-chosen Cowork task independently — one that came from your real work, not a course exercise.

## Challenge

Now reflect on the experience and capture your learnings.

**Step 1: Reflect on these questions:**

- What went well with your Cowork task?
- What would you do differently next time?
- Were your instructions specific enough, or did Cowork miss the mark somewhere?
- What surprised you about how Cowork handled the task?

**Step 2: Create a "Cowork Task Template" file.**

Ask Claude (in this course session) to help you create a file in `workspace/` called `cowork-task-template.md`. This template should include:

- Your personal best practices for writing Cowork instructions
- At least 3 tips or instruction patterns you've learned so far
- A reusable template structure you can copy-paste for future Cowork tasks
- Any "gotchas" or things to avoid

This template is for you — make it practical and specific to your work.

**Step 3: Save it.**

Have Claude save the template to `workspace/cowork-task-template.md`.

> **Before you're done — Question It:** What did Claude assume? What's missing? What could break?

**Success criteria:** Created a Cowork task template file in `workspace/` that contains at least 3 tips or instruction patterns based on your experience.
