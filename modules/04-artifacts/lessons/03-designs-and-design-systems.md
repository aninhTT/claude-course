# Lesson 4.03: Designs & Design Systems

## Learn

A **Design** is a visual layout on a canvas: a one-pager, a flyer, a landing page mockup, an event invite, a Slack or social graphic, an ad. You describe what you want, Claude lays it out, and you refine it.

> Already comfortable with this topic? Skip ahead anytime with `skip` followed by the next lesson number.

### Working With a Design

You can change a design three ways:
- **Ask in the conversation** — "Make the headline bigger," "Try a version with a photo on the left"
- **Comment on the canvas** — click a spot and leave a note right where the change should go
- **Ask for variations** — "Give me three different layouts," then pick the one you like

When it's done, you can export it (PDF, PowerPoint, Google Slides, or HTML) or send it to tools like Canva. If a design is a mockup of something that needs to be built for real — say, a web page — Claude Code can pick it up from there.

> 💡 **Used Claude Design before?** The same tools now live in any conversation. The standalone Claude Design site (claude.ai/design) closes on December 14, 2026.

### Design Systems: The Key Idea

Here's what makes designs (and decks) look on-brand without any effort from you: a **design system**.

A design system is your brand, packaged for Claude — colors, fonts, spacing, logos, and components like buttons and cards. When a design or deck uses a design system, Claude pulls from it automatically. You don't pick colors or fonts; it's already done.

**You *can* add your own design system.** Claude can build one from brand files, uploads, or a codebase.

**But usually, your design team should add it.** A design system works best when one team owns it, keeps it current, and everyone else uses the same one. That's how a whole company stays consistent.

### At Thumbtack: Thumbprint Is Already There

Thumbtack's design team has already added our **Thumbprint** brand to Claude, and it's set as the company default. So you don't need to set anything up. When you create a design or a deck, just say:

> "Use the Thumbprint design."

Claude pulls from the design system, and everything comes out branded.

> 💡 **How it reads vs. how it looks:** Remember the Clean Draft skill you copied in Module 3? Skills like that help with how things are *written and structured*. The design system handles how things *look*. Use both together for content that's clear and on-brand.

### When to Use Design vs. Slides vs. Docs

| If you need... | Use |
|----------------|-----|
| Something people read top to bottom | **Docs** |
| Something you present, one idea per screen | **Slides** |
| Something people glance at, or a single visual | **Design** |

## Practice

Make a branded design for something real.

**Step 1: Pick something visual you need.** Ideas:
- A one-pager explaining your team's work or a new process
- A Slack announcement graphic for an upcoming event or launch
- A flyer or invite for a team event
- A simple mockup of a page or screen you've been describing in words

**Step 2: Ask for it — and name the brand.** For example:

> "Make a one-page Design announcing our team's new intake process. Audience: other teams who send us requests. Use the Thumbprint design."

**Step 3: Check that the brand came through.** Do the colors, fonts, and overall look match Thumbtack's brand? If something looks off, say so and ask Claude to fix it.

**Step 4: Change one thing with a canvas comment.** Click directly on the design and leave a note about what to change.

**Success criteria:** Created a design for a real need using the Thumbprint design system, confirmed it looks on-brand, and made at least one change with a canvas comment.

## Challenge

**Three directions, one winner.** Ask Claude for three noticeably different versions of your design — different layouts, different emphasis, or different tone — all using Thumbprint.

Then:
1. Pick the strongest one and explain why in a sentence or two.
2. Apply Thumbprint to the deck you made in Lesson 4.02, if it isn't already, so your doc, deck, and design all match.
3. Export the winner in the format you'd actually use.

> **Before you're done — Question It:** What did Claude assume? What's missing? What could break?

**Success criteria:** Compared three branded variations, chose one with a clear reason, and exported it.
